\begin{abstract}
We predict whether a person reports a heart attack or coronary heart disease (MICHD)
from 321 answers to the 2015 BRFSS health survey, where only 8.8\% of 328,135 people
are positive. We encode the survey column by column, compare 13 methods, and submit
an ensemble of two Newton logistic regressions, one with feature products and one followed by boosted
trees: F1 = 0.4356 in 5-fold cross-validation, 0.446 on AIcrowd. An ablation shows
that the threshold and the encoding matter more than the model. We also find, and
keep out, a column that reveals the label.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The BRFSS is a yearly US telephone health survey~\cite{brfss2015}. From its 2015
answers (328,135 training and 109,379 test people) we predict MICHD: the person was told they had a heart attack, angina or
coronary heart disease.

With 8.8\% positives, answering ``healthy'' for everybody is 91\% accurate, so
accuracy is useless. The challenge uses F1, the harmonic mean of
\emph{precision} (the share of flagged people who are positive) and \emph{recall}
(the share of positive people we flag). Flagging everybody gives F1 = 0.1623, the
best rule on one question (self-rated general health) 0.3088.

We contribute a survey-aware pipeline with an ablation of its steps, 13 methods
compared on the same folds, an ensemble that beats the best single model in every
fold, and a measured label leak.

\section{Models and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data and preparation}
\label{sec:data}

Our exploratory analysis (\texttt{experiments/EDA.md}) found three problems.
\emph{Codes are not values:} 7 usually means ``don't know'', but ``retired'' for the
employment question, so we wrote one rule per column from the codebook.
\emph{Missing values are everywhere and informative:} an average column is 47\% missing, because of optional modules and of skip logic (a follow-up question is
not asked when it does not apply); people who answered the diabetes module have 21.9\%
MICHD, the others 7.9\%. \emph{Many columns repeat others.} Train and test are
distributed alike.

Our pipeline fits every statistic (medians, categories, informative missing values)
on the training rows of each fold only.
\emph{Clean:} special codes become ``missing'' or 0, each answer gets one unit, and
skipped questions are filled with ``no''.
\emph{Select:} drop 41 columns that do not describe the person (ids, dates, survey
weights) and the 30 duplicates, then keep 124 of the 236 left with four rules. The
main one drops 89 columns whose \emph{mutual information} with the label is below
1~millibit, i.e.\ knowing them removes almost none of the 431~millibits of uncertainty
about the label; the others mostly keep one column per small optional module.
\emph{Fill:} add an ``is missing'' column per group of columns missing together
when missingness shifts the MICHD rate by $\geq$3 points; impute the median (numbers)
or the mode (yes/no, categories).
\emph{Encode:} yes/no becomes 0/1; categories, ordinal answers whose risk is not
monotone (e.g.\ smoking status) and the binned days of poor physical health become one
0/1 column per value with at least 500 people (\emph{one-hot}); then every column is
standardized. This gives 336 inputs.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}

\emph{Required methods} (\texttt{implementations.py}): linear regression by gradient
descent (GD) and by stochastic GD, least squares, ridge regression, and logistic
regression by GD with and without the penalty $\lambda\|w\|^2$. Linear models are fitted
to labels $\pm1$. Least squares uses a pseudo-inverse from the eigendecomposition of
$X^\top X$, which is singular when columns are collinear.

\emph{Other optimizers} of the regularized logistic loss: adaptive-step GD
(Barzilai--Borwein~\cite{barzilai1988}), UniXGrad~\cite{kavis2019unixgrad},
Sophia~\cite{liu2023sophia}, and \emph{Newton's method}, which uses the curvature of
the loss to set each step: about 10 steps reach the minimum, with no step size.

\emph{Trees} on 32 bins per column~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}: a random
forest~\cite{breiman2001} and gradient boosting~\cite{friedman2001}, i.e.\ small trees
fitted one after the other to the errors left by the previous ones (split gain of
XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost}, half of the rows and columns per
tree~\cite{friedman2002}).

\emph{Logistic boosting} fits the logistic regression with Newton
($\lambda = 10^{-4}$), then starts the boosting from its log-odds $x^\top w$ instead of
a constant, like the base margin of XGBoost. The linear part captures the additive
effects (age, general health) exactly; 400 trees (depth 3, learning rate 0.05, $\geq$100
people per leaf) learn what is left. It is a simple combination, judged by the
ablation.

\emph{Newton with expanded features} adds squares of the non-binary columns,
$\log(1 + x - \min x)$ of the continuous and count ones, and the products of all pairs of the 20
columns most correlated with the label (190 products, chosen inside each fold):
619 columns, $\lambda = 10^{-6}$.

\emph{Ensemble and threshold.} The submission averages the two probabilities,
$p = a\,p_{\text{Newton}} + (1 - a)\,p_{\text{boosting}}$ with $a = 0.35$, and
predicts MICHD when $p \geq t$. With 8.8\% positives, $t = 0.5$ flags almost
nobody, so $t$ maximizes F1 on training predictions, never on the scored rows
($t = 0.206$).

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

All experiments share 5 stratified folds (seed 1): pipeline, model and $t$ are fitted
on four folds, F1 is measured on the fifth. We report mean $\pm$ standard deviation
(about 0.002, so smaller differences are noise) and in how many folds a variant beats
its reference. Hyperparameters were searched in stages on small grids. The ensemble
weight $a \in \{0, 0.05, \ldots, 1\}$ and $t$ are chosen on out-of-fold probabilities;
both models are then refitted on all training rows. \texttt{python run.py}
rebuilds the submitted file byte for byte in about 11.5 minutes.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\emph{Baselines (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).} Our preparation improves each required
method by 0.0027 to 0.0088. Logistic beats linear regression by about 0.01; three
optimizers of the same loss end within 0.0005, while Sophia, the only stochastic one,
stays at 0.3928. Trees alone do worse.

\emph{Ablation (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}).} The threshold dwarfs everything: at
$t = 0.5$, logistic boosting scores 0.2395 instead of 0.4338. Among data steps only one-hot
encoding is large; the selection adds nothing measurable, and each cleaning step not
shown changes F1 by less than 0.001. Squares and logarithms change nothing (0.4323 to
0.4324), products do not help logistic boosting ($-0.0005$), and clinical features
(counts of conditions, age $\times$ sex, \ldots) lower F1 by 0.0004 to 0.0013.

\emph{Submissions (Table~\ref{tab:submissions}).} The ensemble weight is stable
across folds (0.35 to 0.40). Our best online score, 0.452, used the leaking column discussed
below: the test performance of our submission is 0.446.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Where the gain comes from.} All reasonable models end between 0.43 and
0.435: the limit is probably the information in the answers, not the model. Products
and trees give Newton the same +0.0015 and their gains do not combine, so they likely
capture the same interactions. We did not check that the ensemble's parts err on different people,
or that GD lags Newton by not converging.

\emph{A column that reveals the label.} \texttt{HAREHAB1} asks about rehabilitation
\emph{after a heart attack}, so it is asked only to people who had one: all 773
training rows where it was asked are positive. As a feature it adds 0.0075 in all
folds, and as a rule it lifts the ensemble to 0.4414. We do not use it: it reveals the
label, and it exists only after the event.

\emph{Limitations.} The selection rules saw the labels of all training rows, so CV
is slightly optimistic (unmeasured; the selection itself adds only 0.0002). The data
ablation uses Newton, not the ensemble; $\lambda = 10^{-6}$ is at its grid edge; and
there is no nested CV.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

Careful encoding and an ensemble of two Newton logistic regressions give F1 =
0.4356 in CV and 0.446 on AIcrowd. Threshold and encoding matter most, and a leaking
column would add score, not usefulness.
